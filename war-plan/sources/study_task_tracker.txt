- If there is an exam on a given day and another exam immediately the next day, then after finishing the current day's exam, the remaining study time should be focused on the next day's exam.
- Morning vs afternoon exam timing matters when constructing the daily schedule.
- All currently listed non-practical exams above are in the afternoon; practical exam times are currently uncertain.
- Practical exams do NOT require dedicated full revision days/blocks in the plan.
- For practical exams, only a brief look/review shortly before the exam is needed.

=== STUDY-CAPACITY / EXAM-RUN-IN RULES ===
- On any day with an exam, plan for approximately 4 hours of study time outside the exam itself.
- On a day with no exam, plan for approximately 11 hours of study time.
- Because the currently listed non-practical exams are in the afternoon, the previous day does NOT need to be dominated by that subject; the morning of the exam can be used for focused final review.
- In the 3–7 days before an exam, study allocation and past-paper selection should increasingly lean toward the upcoming exam.
- Past-paper volume should be front-loaded: do more full papers several days before the exam rather than leaving a large number for the final 1–2 days.
- In the final 1–2 days before an exam, reduce full past-paper volume and shift toward targeted review, corrections, weak topics, formulas/definitions, and selected questions.
- For an afternoon exam, use the morning mainly for concise, focused review rather than heavy full-paper work.
- If two written exams are on consecutive days, after finishing the first exam use the remaining study time to pivot toward the next day's paper, while keeping in mind that the next exam is also in the afternoon.
